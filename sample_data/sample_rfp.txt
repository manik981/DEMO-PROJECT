1. PROJECT OVERVIEW

The City of Riverton seeks a vendor to design, build and launch a public Digital Permit
Portal that replaces the current paper-based permitting process. Residents and local
businesses currently submit permit applications in person at the municipal office,
which creates queues, lost paperwork and limited visibility into application status.

The business objective is to reduce in-person permit submissions by 70 percent within
12 months of launch, shorten average application processing time and give staff a
single digital queue. The portal will be used by residents, contractors, small business
owners and City permitting staff. The City's communications team will publish guidance
content.

2. FUNCTIONAL REQUIREMENTS

FR-1. Applicants must be able to create an account, submit a permit application online
and save a draft before submission.
FR-2. The system must support at least six permit types at launch: residential
renovation, fence, signage, food service, short-term rental and special event.
FR-3. Applicants must upload supporting documents in PDF, PNG and JPEG format. Each
file must be no larger than 10 MB.
FR-4. The portal must calculate and collect permit fees online using the City's existing
Stripe merchant account. A receipt must be emailed automatically after payment.
FR-5. Applicants must receive email and SMS notifications when an application is
received, when additional information is requested and when a decision is issued.
FR-6. City staff must have a review dashboard showing the queue, application details,
uploaded documents and a complete audit history of status changes.
FR-7. The dashboard must support the following roles: applicant, intake clerk, permit
reviewer, supervisor and system administrator.
FR-8. Supervisors must be able to reassign applications and export a monthly report as
a PDF.
FR-9. The public must be able to search approved permits by address, permit type and
date.
FR-10. The system must retain all application records and audit logs for seven years.
FR-11. Applicants must be able to request an inspection online and view inspection
results.
FR-12. The portal must display a "user-friendly" interface and provide "reasonable
response times" for all pages.

3. NON-FUNCTIONAL REQUIREMENTS

NFR-1. Availability: the public portal must be available 99.9 percent of the time,
measured monthly, excluding scheduled maintenance.
NFR-2. Capacity: the system must support 500 concurrent users. During peak periods the
system must support 1,000 concurrent users.
NFR-3. Accessibility: the public-facing portal must conform to WCAG 2.1 Level AA.
NFR-4. Security: all data must be encrypted in transit and at rest. The vendor must
follow "appropriate security practices."
NFR-5. Privacy: the system must collect only the personal information necessary to
process permits and must publish a privacy notice.
NFR-6. Performance: each page must load in under two seconds for 95 percent of requests.
Elsewhere in this document, the City states that "all pages must respond within five
seconds."
NFR-7. Maintainability: the vendor must provide source code, deployment documentation
and administrator training at handover.

4. TECHNICAL REQUIREMENTS

TR-1. The solution must integrate with the City's Tyler Munis ERP for permit fee
posting and general ledger reconciliation.
TR-2. Single sign-on for City staff must use the City's Microsoft Entra ID (Azure AD)
tenant. Public applicants will use local accounts.
TR-3. The City prefers deployment on AWS in the us-east-1 region. The vendor may
propose an alternative only with a documented reason.
TR-4. The City has previously used PostgreSQL and is comfortable with it. No database
is mandated.
TR-5. A React frontend is preferred but not required.
TR-6. The solution must send SMS through a commercial messaging provider. The specific
provider has not been selected.
TR-7. The City requires a disaster recovery plan, but recovery time and recovery point
objectives have not been defined.

5. DATA, INTEGRATIONS AND OPERATIONS

The vendor will migrate open permit records from the current access database. The City
estimates that the database contains several thousand records but has not completed an
inventory. Data quality is unknown.

The selected vendor will provide a support desk during business hours. The service
level for incident response has not been agreed. The City has not decided whether the
vendor or City IT will own cloud operations after launch.

Payment card data must not be stored on City systems. The Stripe integration must be
reviewed by the City's finance team.

6. TIMELINE AND BUDGET

The City expects the portal to launch within six months of contract signature. Phase 1
must go live by 1 June 2026. The full solution, including inspections and public search,
will launch in Q4 2026; however, the City also states that all functionality in this RFP
is required at launch. The implementation budget has not been approved and will be
confirmed after vendor selection.

7. EVALUATION

Proposals will be evaluated on relevant experience, technical approach and price.
The City will hold demonstrations with shortlisted vendors. Accessibility testing
responsibilities are not specified. The City expects the vendor to propose a project
plan and a training approach.
